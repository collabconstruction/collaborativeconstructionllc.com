### Profitability definitions for confirmation

- Gross profit per job = recognized project revenue less consistently defined direct project costs. Include materials, subcontractors and attributable labor; document whether owner project labor is included. Excludes general overhead under this definition.
- Profit per owner-hour = explicitly named profit numerator divided by recorded owner hours. Prefer reporting gross contribution per owner-hour and fully costed net profit per owner-hour as separate, labeled measures where inputs support both. Do not call either an hourly wage. Zero or missing hours make the ratio not calculable.
- Profit per job-week = the same explicitly named profit numerator divided by consistently measured project duration. Use a declared start/end rule and completed jobs for realized comparisons. For a service group use aggregate profit divided by aggregate valid job-weeks; overlapping projects mean this is not company profit per calendar week.

Declare owner labor treatment and overhead allocation once. If owner labor is valued at an hourly cost, apply it consistently to advisory and construction. Do not deduct it twice through payroll/direct labor and an additional owner-time charge. Distinguish actual recorded cost from estimated opportunity cost, and do not infer a rate from missing data. Reconcile job totals to the same accounting period/basis before ranking services. Revenue, gross profit, net profit and cash receipts are different measures.

Also record available crew/subcontractor capacity, owner supervision capacity for overlapping jobs and working-capital needs/payment timing. No project acceptance promise should be generated from a profitability ranking alone.
