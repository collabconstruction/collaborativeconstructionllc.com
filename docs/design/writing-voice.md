# Collaborative Construction website writing

Owner direction, October 14, 2026: professional, direct construction language. Avoid corny slogans, forced familiarity and theatrical metaphors.

## Rules for every copy edit

- Lead with the service, location or practical next step. Use familiar construction terms.
- Use short complete sentences. Explain scope, responsibilities and what information the homeowner needs to supply.
- Prefer “Discuss your project,” “View projects,” and “Residential construction and renovation.”
- Avoid “in the room,” “opposite chair,” “other side of the table,” “six ways in,” “planning something like this,” “dreams into reality,” and self-referential explanations of the company name.
- Do not imply adversarial relationships with builders. Explain independent oversight and reporting responsibilities factually.
- Do not invent response times, guarantees, prices, project ownership, credentials, capacity or customer claims. Preserve verified factual meaning.
- Name services consistently. Separate general contracting, owner's project management and Clerk of the Works when their responsibilities differ.
- Check headings, paragraphs, buttons and form messages as one visitor journey. Remove paragraphs that only repeat a heading.
- Read copy aloud, then check it at phone width. Replace anything that sounds like a slogan generator or could be interpreted as threatening or intrusive.

## Approved examples

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| Built with you in the room. | Residential construction & renovation. |
| Planning Something Like This? | Discuss Your Project |
| Tell us what you have in mind and we'll talk through how it gets built, what it involves, and what to expect. | Contact us to discuss your project scope, location and timing. |
| Six ways in | Our services |
| We watch it for you | We oversee the work |

## October 14 presentation update

Use construction/service language instead of a Cape-focused marketing identity. Keep geographic details where they explain an actual project, About background, contact address or availability discussion. Preserve canonical URLs and truthful service-area facts. Avoid decorative dashes and intimate/adversarial metaphors. Center section introductions while keeping long technical content readable.
